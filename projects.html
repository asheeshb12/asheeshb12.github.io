Major Projects Led or Contributed To

Uncertainty-Aware ML for Deep Convection Initiation

GRL 2026 • Lead Author

Predicting the precise onset of deep convection remains a fundamental challenge in numerical weather forecasting. In this project, we designed uncertainty-aware machine learning frameworks to investigate under what specific atmospheric thermodynamic and dynamic conditions trigger predictions are most reliable. The models were evaluated against high-resolution DOE ARM Southern Great Plains (SGP) observations and kilometer-scale hydroclimate reanalysis datasets.

Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) Deep Learning DOE ARM Observations Kilometer-Scale Reanalysis Python / xarray

Interpretable Latent Space of Storm Morphology

Deep Generative AI • AGU 2026

Traditional meteorological metrics often condense complex storm structures into simple scalars (e.g., echo tops or total precipitation), losing critical morphological context. We developed deep generative architectures—specifically Two-Head β-Variational Autoencoders (TwoHeadVAE) and Vision Transformers (ViT)—paired with Pareto-front multi-objective optimization to learn disentangled, physically interpretable latent representations of 2D radar-observed storm systems from NOAA MRMS datasets.

Coupled WRF-CAMx Planetary Boundary Layer & Ozone Dynamics

Atmos. Environ. 2026 • Best Poster Award

Investigated offshore planetary boundary layer height (PBLH) and photochemical surface ozone formation during the NASA/TCEQ TRACER-AQ field campaign in the Houston-Galveston region. Leveraged coupled WRF and CAMx numerical modeling to assess how complex cloud conditions, marine-terrestrial interfaces, and local meteorology affect air quality simulations.

WRF (Numerical Weather Prediction) CAMx (Air Quality Model) SODAR RASS & UAVs TRACER-AQ Field Data

PACE OCI, TEMPO & TROPOMI Satellite NO₂ Validation

Remote Sensing • Under Review

Conducted an extensive evaluation of newly available total column NO₂ measurements from NASA’s PACE Ocean Color Instrument (OCI), comparing them against ground-based Pandora spectrometer observations across the contiguous United States, as well as geostationary TEMPO and polar-orbiting TROPOMI satellite retrievals.

Climato-Environmental Disease Mapping & Groundwater Analysis

Nepal • Published Research

At Tribhuvan University's NARERL laboratory, developed high-resolution spatial vulnerability maps for vector-borne disease transmission (malaria risk zones) across Nepal based on climate and environmental variables. Additionally, conducted spatial modeling of groundwater table fluctuations in eastern Nepal, published in the Journal of Nepal Physical Society.
