I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Earth and Atmospheric Sciences at the University of Houston, advised by Dr. Youtong Zheng. My work lies at the intersection of atmospheric dynamics, scientific machine learning, and high-performance computing.

My doctoral research focuses on developing physically interpretable deep learning models (Variational Autoencoders, Vision Transformers) and uncertainty quantification (UQ) frameworks to investigate deep convection initiation, storm morphology, and planetary boundary layer meteorology.

Before joining as a PhD student at the University of Houston, I completed my Bachelor's and Master's degrees in Physics at Tribhuvan University in Nepal. At the National Atmospheric Resource and Environmental Research Laboratory (NARERL), I conducted air pollution dispersion modeling across the Pokhara Valley and Dharan–Biratnagar corridor and developed climate-driven epidemiological risk maps. Subsequently, I served as a Physics Instructor at Koshi Saint James Higher Secondary School (2021–2023), teaching foundational and advanced physics while mentoring aspiring science students.
